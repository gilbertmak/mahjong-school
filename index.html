What are the tiles?

The set breaks into three counting suits — Dots, Bamboo and Characters — each numbered one to nine with four copies apiece. Above those sit the honours: four winds and three dragons. Then eight bonus tiles, the flowers and seasons. Tap a tile to read its name, its Chinese, and what it does.

How do you win?

A finished hand is fourteen tiles split into four sets and a single pair. A set is either a run of three in one suit (a chow), three of a kind (a pung), or four (a kong). And before you can call it, the hand has to be worth at least 3 faan from the patterns below — so they're worth learning.

How do you draw & deal?

There's a bit of setup first: shuffle the tiles, stack them into a wall, crack it open with the dice, then hand out 13 to each player — with one spare going to the dealer for a starting 14.

Pick the dealer

Everyone throws two dice. Whoever rolls highest takes the East seat for the opening hand; if there's a tie, only the tied players roll again.

Seating

Each seat takes a wind — East, with South to its right, then West, then North. Play runs anti-clockwise around the table.

Rotation

Win as East and you keep the seat; otherwise East passes to the next player. The round's prevailing wind shifts once every four full go-arounds.

1
Build the wall

Shuffle all 144 tiles face-down, then build a square wall in front of the four players — two tiles high, 34 stacks to a side.

2
Break the wall

The dealer rolls once more and counts that many seats round from East; that seat's wall is where you break in. Count the same number of stacks from its right end — dealing starts there.

Why? It scrambles the starting point, so no one can read the wall ahead of time.
3
Deal 13 to each

Going anti-clockwise, each player takes tiles in blocks of four. Three passes leaves everyone on twelve; one more tile each makes thirteen — except East takes two, opening with fourteen.

4
Set aside bonuses

Got a flower or season? Flip it face-up and pull a fresh tile from the tail of the wall (the "dead wall"). Keep going until nobody is holding a bonus tile.

It works the same mid-game: the moment you draw a flower, show it and replace it from the dead wall.
打

What can you do each turn?

On your go you draw one tile and let one go — that's the whole turn. In between, anyone can break in with a call: three of them simply claim a tile (chow, pung, kong), while the fourth — mahjong — ends the hand.

How is it scored?

Hong Kong scoring uses faan (番) — doubling units. Most tables require ≥3 faan to declare, and patterns stack. A hand can score from several at once. Hands of 10 faan and up usually pay out as a "limit" hand.

Minimum to win: 3 faan, typical. Confirm before you sit — some friendly tables play 1 faan, some 5. Whatever the table agrees on beats any printed chart.

Now you play.

Take the East seat — you're the dealer — against three bots. You see your own tiles; theirs stay hidden, same as a real table. Draw each turn, then tap a tile to throw it. If someone discards something you can pung, chow or win on, you'll get the option. When the hand ends, you'll see exactly which patterns paid out.
